What changed, and what didn’t
  • Overview: the slogan hero, the tilted note and the three stat cards are gone. The offer (“Needs you”) comes first. The application list is sorted by what needs action. “2 unread companion updates” is now one row instead of a stat card. Every status keeps its “Simulated” tag, and the queue-position note stays word for word.
  • Find: the title is plain (“Find centres”). The search box comes before “Your centres”, which is now a horizontal strip. The result card keeps every label, licence, source and date line, plus “capacity, not availability”, “Availability not reported” and “Not part of the CareQ demo; contact the centre directly.”, grouped into name → distance → ages → facts → availability → sources.
  • The “Prototype” banner, notifications, the Track “Offer” badge and Ask CareQ all stay. The phone nav row keeps the same items and only gets a clearer active state. Navigation, data and workflows don’t change.

Why Nunito Sans

  • It has a larger x-height, so 14–16px text and long centre names stay readable on phones. Lato looks smaller at the same size.
  • Its slightly rounded ends feel warmer than Lato, without looking childish like a fully rounded face.
  • It is a variable font with weights 400–800 and an optical-size axis, so headings and captions are each tuned to their size from a single family file.
  • Lato is very common in 2010s templates and government sites, which are the looks the brief says to avoid.

Buttons

10px corners, 44px tall (36px compact variant on desktop tables only), and a coral focus ring with an offset.

Primary, secondary, tertiary, destructive, disabled and keyboard focus. Arrows appear only on buttons that move you to another screen. When pressed, a button shrinks slightly to 98% and gets darker.
